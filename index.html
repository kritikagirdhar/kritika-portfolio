RRapido · Concept study · Ride-hailing

Minimising app switching to increase ride conversion

Riders on short, time-sensitive trips keep two or three ride apps open. Rapido loses the ride the moment a rider stops trusting its wait time.

Problem

Riders switch to Ola or Uber while waiting for a captain, so requests don't convert into rides.

Solution

A live wait-time estimate, plus wait rewards and a visible captain reputation score.

Measure

Ride completion, cancellations, retention, CSAT and reward redemption.

01

Approach

Goal: enhance the user experience and decision-making in ride-hailing by reducing app switching.

1

Identify pain points

Understand why users switch between ride-hailing apps through surveys, interviews and behaviour analysis.

2

Focus on experience

Make fare and ride information more accessible and transparent inside the app.

3

Develop solutions

Design features that offer value beyond simple fare comparison.

4

Test and iterate

Pilot with a small user group, then refine using their feedback.

02

03

Define

How might we reduce app switching during booking, so more ride requests become completed rides?

User-reported issues in the highlighted phase

  • Long waits for a captain to be assigned, with low driver availability at peak hours and in cities like Bengaluru and Chennai
  • Captains taking too long to reach pickup, often struggling to find the location
  • Captains demanding more than the quoted fare
  • Cancellation policies that penalise riders unfairly

Root causes

Why the long wait for a captain?

  • Low driver availability nearby
  • Drivers not accepting or cancelling rides
  • Drivers unhappy with the estimated earnings
  • Drivers receiving requests from far away

Why is the captain slow to arrive?

  • Traffic and weather
  • Technical and GPS problems
  • Still finishing the previous ride

Why do captains demand more?

  • Compensation feels too low
  • Extra effort such as waiting or luggage
  • Seasonal supply–demand imbalance
Key insight

Most root causes are logistical and driver-side, so Rapido can't fix them overnight. The solutions instead focus on the rider: reduce the uncertainty of the wait, and give riders a reason to stay even when competitors are one tap away.

05

Design

Solution 1

Average wait time estimate

Real-time, data-driven insight into how long a rider can expect to wait in their area, built from historical data and current demand and supply.

Notifications
Riders can opt in to push or SMS updates when the estimate changes significantly.
Feedback loop
After each ride, riders rate how accurate the estimate was, which refines the prediction model.
Pain points solved
  1. Uncertainty: sets a realistic expectation, reducing anxiety
  2. Trust in the platform
  3. App switching, through clear wait expectations
Solution 2

Wait time rewards and driver reputation

Riders are rewarded for their patience, and drivers are encouraged to stay reliable through a reputation score that riders can see.

Rewards
1 minute of waiting = 10 points; 10 points = ₹5 off the next ride.
Reputation
Riders can downvote a driver who cancels after contacting them. The higher the score, the less likely a driver is to cancel.
Pain points solved
  1. Rider frustration
  2. Driver reliability concerns
  3. Cancellation anxiety, since scores are public
  4. Differentiation from competitors

Objective

Increase average order value (AOV) by 10% to reach profitability through Swiggy Instamart.

Why AOV matters

As average order value rises, unit economics turn profitable. Profit per order = revenue per order − cost per order, and a bigger basket spreads the fixed delivery cost.

Product outcomes

  • Fewer orders: combining orders, while total value holds or grows, optimises operations.
  • Higher total value: total order value moves with AOV, so each order adds more to revenue.

01

Context: the game and the market

Marble Sort is a physics puzzle by Voodoo, launched in October 2025. Players tap boxes to release marbles, which ride a circular conveyor into matching three-slot trays. The conveyor never stops, so a level is lost when it jams.

How the industry earns

  • In growing markets like India, ads bring roughly 55–70% of game revenue. In mature markets, in-app purchases bring 77–90%.
  • Puzzle games earn 53% of all mobile-game ad revenue.
  • Casual games typically split revenue 40–60% between purchases and ads.

Why the first purchase matters

  • Only 2–5% of daily players in most free-to-play games ever spend.
  • The top 1% of players generate over half of revenue.
  • So the first purchase is the hardest step, and the one that opens the door to every later offer.
02

Core loop and where money is made

Tap a box→ Marbles ride the conveyor→ Fill matching trays→ Clear the level, or the conveyor jams→ Reward, ad, or offer

The jam is the emotional peak of every level: the player is one move from winning and wants help now. It is the natural moment for a rewarded ad or a purchase. Today that moment is often followed by a forced ad instead.

Current player flow

→ → → →

Screenshots from my own play-through on iOS, level 960.

What I noticed while playing
  • A No Ads purchase already exists on the home screen, so the question isn't whether to offer ad removal, but how to make a first purchase feel worth it.
  • Boosters are unlocked by rewarded ads (the video badge on each booster). Players who use them are already trading attention for help, which is the strongest sign of buying intent.
  • Forced ads follow every result: a full-screen video plays whether the player has just won or lost. Losing a "Very Hard" level and then sitting through an ad is a double frustration.
  • Ad quality is a risk: the ad shown was for a leveraged trading app offering a "50% deposit bonus", inside a game rated 4+. That is a brand-safety issue as much as a revenue one.
  • Plenty of engagement hooks: three live events, timed chests and a win-streak meter. These are good places for an offer that doesn't interrupt play.

Problem

Context: how UPI scams changed

Lost to cyber fraud, 2025₹22,495 cr
Complaints, 202528.15 lakh

LocalCircles survey

  • Collect requests removed: NPCI stopped P2P collect requests from 1 October 2025, closing a major scam route. Every P2P payment is now started by the payer.
  • Scams moved to persuasion: investment scams cause about three-quarters of losses, and "digital arrest" scams another 9%. In both, the victim sends the money themselves.
  • Speed cuts both ways: UPI moves money in seconds, but prompt reporting can still freeze funds. The government's reporting system saved ₹7,130 crore this way.

03

Define

How might we stop users from sending money to scammers, without adding friction to the everyday payments that make UPI great?

  • Constraint: the vast majority of payments are genuine, so any check must be targeted.
  • Constraint: the user, not a fraudster, is the one tapping "Pay". Blocking alone won't work; the user must be convinced.

MVP: pause and check plus one-tap reporting. Family Guard and on-call detection follow in phase 2, once the risk model is proven.

05

Design: concept screens

Concept mock-ups for a generic UPI app.

When does the check appear?
  • First payment to this UPI ID, and the amount is unusual for this user
  • The payee has been reported by other users or appears in suspect registries
  • The payee is a new account receiving many first-time payments (a mule pattern)
  • The user is on an active phone call while paying (phase 2, on-device, with consent)

Verified merchant QR codes are exempt, so shop payments stay instant.

07

Risks and mitigations

RiskScammers coach victims: "You'll see a warning, just ignore it."

MitigationThe check asks the user's reason, and names this tactic in the warning itself.

RiskAlert fatigue from too many checks.

MitigationStrict risk thresholds, merchant QR exemption, and a cap on checks per user.

RiskPrivacy concerns about call detection.

MitigationOn-device only, opt-in, and only a yes/no "on call" signal.

RiskGenuine urgent payments get delayed.

MitigationThe wait applies only to the highest-risk tier, with Family Guard as a way to approve instantly.
